Start a separate chat inside your Project for each main task. These names are suggestions, not special settings. State which role and materials to use each time; do not assume every earlier detail has been picked up correctly.

Use the fictional profile in the walkthrough first. For your own search, remove unnecessary identifying details before supplying information.

1. My experience and strengths

Help me build an evidence list for my job search. Ask one question at a time about work, volunteering, study or caring responsibilities I choose to discuss. Help me recognise transferable skills. Do not invent achievements or ask for private details.

Save a short, checked summary of your experience for later tasks. A transferable skill is something useful in different settings, such as organising a rota or explaining a process.

2. Understanding a job advert

Using the advert I supply next, explain the role in plain English. Separate essential and desirable requirements. Make a table with each requirement, evidence from my supplied profile and any gap or question. Do not assume I meet a requirement because I have a related skill.

Check the original advert for deadlines, location, working hours and application instructions.

3. Tailoring my CV

Suggest changes to my CV for this role using only the evidence I have supplied. Show the original wording alongside your suggested wording and explain why each change helps. Mark missing evidence as [detail needed]. Do not add results, qualifications or software skills I have not confirmed.

Keep your own master CV. Read every change before using it.

4. A covering letter or supporting statement

Using this advert and my checked evidence, draft a short supporting statement in UK English. Make it specific and natural. Follow the word limit I provide, tell me the word count and flag anything I need to confirm. Do not claim experience I do not have.

Follow the employer's instructions, including any rules about AI assistance. You should be able to explain every sentence.

5. Recruiter conversations

Draft a friendly reply asking about working hours, location and the salary range. Use placeholders for names. Do not include my address, current salary or other private information. Give me a draft to review; do not send it.

Avoid pasting whole private email chains. Describe the question or provide a short anonymised extract instead.

6. Interview practice

Act as a practice interviewer for the role in the supplied advert. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. Then give kind, specific feedback on clarity, relevance and evidence. Help me use STAR: Situation, Task, Action and Result. Do not invent a result for me.

Practise explaining the answer aloud without reading it. The aim is confidence in your own experience.

7. My next steps

Based only on the status information below, help me plan three realistic job-search actions this week. Separate draft applications, submitted applications and interviews. Do not mark anything as submitted unless I confirm it.

Keep your own simple record: role, employer, deadline, status and next action. Check dates against the original source.

If the answer goes wrong

Try: “Which part of my supplied information supports that claim?” or “You have added a skill I do not have. Remove it and revise the draft honestly.”

You can also ask for a shorter explanation, a concrete example or one step at a time.
