THE JOB-SEARCH GUIDE / 08

Your whole job-search journey

From the first recruiter call to the decision that suits you.

One ChatGPT Project can help organise the whole job-search journey: finding a role or taking a recruiter call, preparing applications, rehearsing interviews, reflecting on feedback and comparing offers. Keep separate chats for focused tasks, with a short, checked overview connecting them.

The habit that makes it useful: update it after every important conversation. Bring back what changed, what you learnt and what happens next. You can speak an update where voice or dictation is available, rather than type everything.

These are suggested workflows, not a tested end-to-end recruitment service. AI feedback can help you practise; it cannot predict an interviewer or guarantee a better outcome.

1. Give each opportunity its own record

Use labels such as Role A — administrator and Role B — customer services. Keep the advert, CV version, application status, interview arrangements and feedback associated with the right role. This helps avoid using one employer's details in another application.

Keep a chat called Search overview and updates and maintain a short dated summary. Save a checked copy somewhere you control. Bring the relevant summary into a new task or add a current version as a Project source where supported.

Help me keep a current job-search overview using only facts I confirm.
For each role, record: role label, employer or anonymised label,
source and date checked, application stage, CV version used,
interview format and date, feedback source, open questions,
deadline and next action. Keep unknown details marked unknown.

Separate confirmed employer/recruiter information, my recollection,
my impressions and your suggestions. Never mark an application sent,
an interview confirmed or an offer accepted unless I confirm it.
Before helping with a role, summarise the latest information you can
access and ask me to correct gaps or conflicting versions.

A Project keeps related sources and chats together, but do not assume every detail from every chat is always available or recalled correctly. Check the context before each important task. If ChatGPT cannot find a detail, supply the latest checked summary instead of asking it to guess. See the official Projects guide.

2. Start with a vacancy or recruiter call

You can begin with Job Sweep, an advert you found yourself or a recruiter approaching you. Before a call, prepare a short account of your relevant experience and questions about responsibilities, location, pay, hours and the hiring process. After the call, update the record with what was actually said.

Use the CV and application prompts to prepare a tailored CV and supporting statement for each role. Track multiple applications separately. Check every claim, employer instruction and deadline, then submit the application yourself and record that it was sent.

3. Practise the interview you are expecting

Keep a chat such as Role A — interview practice. Supply the latest advert, checked experience, interview invitation details and relevant recruiter feedback. Leave out private contact details and unnecessary names; roles such as hiring manager, team lead and HR interviewer are usually enough.

Adapt practice to a recruiter screening call, a telephone or online interview, or an in-person panel. Use confirmed information about the format, length, presentation or exercise. For online interviews, check your real camera, microphone and connection separately; for an in-person interview, plan the journey too.

Where ChatGPT Voice is available, start a voice conversation in the relevant chat and answer aloud. Voice is a two-way conversation; dictation turns your speech into a written message. Availability and controls vary by account, device and workspace. If you cannot use voice in that chat, paste a brief into a supported voice chat or practise aloud and enter a short summary for feedback. See the official Voice guide.

Run a practice interview for [Role A] using the current brief I supply.
First summarise the role, interview format, confirmed panel roles,
my evidence and the latest feedback. Ask me to correct missing details.

Simulate a hiring manager, a team lead and an HR interviewer,
or the panel roles I confirm. Announce the role asking each question.
These are fictional interview perspectives: do not impersonate real
people or claim to know their personalities or actual questions.

Ask one question at a time and wait for my spoken or typed answer.
Use follow-up questions where useful. Do not invent experience for me.
For this first round, give feedback after each answer on relevance,
clarity, evidence and structure, with one thing to improve.
If you have only a transcript, do not claim to assess my eye contact,
body language or vocal delivery. Keep feedback grounded in what
you can actually observe. Do not infer suitability from my accent.

At the end, give two strengths, two gaps and one answer to practise
again. Your feedback is coaching, not a hiring verdict or prediction.

You are rehearsing different panel perspectives, not recreating the actual people. Names in an invitation do not reveal what someone will ask or think. One assistant can take turns as several panel roles; this does not promise separate voices for each person.

Try another round with feedback only at the end for a more continuous rehearsal. Improve the substance of one answer, then answer it again in your own words. Use STAR — Situation, Task, Action, Result — when it helps; avoid memorising a script or adding a result you cannot support.

4. Update after each call or interview

Soon afterwards, open the relevant chat and give a short spoken or typed debrief. This is your own account afterwards, not a request to record the actual recruiter or interview panel. Only share what is necessary and permitted; omit private or confidential details.

Here is my update for [Role A], dated [date].
What happened: [call or interview and main points].
Questions I remember: [brief notes].
My answers and reflections: [what went well and what was difficult].
Feedback actually received: [anonymised summary, who provided it,
and date; say 'none yet' if applicable].
New confirmed details: [role, terms, next stage or deadline].
Next action: [what I need to do and when].

Summarise this for me to check before treating it as the current
record. Keep my impressions separate from feedback actually received.
List corrections to the previous overview and one practice priority.
Do not assume anything was sent or agreed unless I have said so.

Check any voice-generated text or summary, especially dates, figures, names and whether a sentence says something was or was not agreed. Correct mistakes, then keep the checked overview current. ChatGPT does not automatically know what happened in a real call or interview: you need to tell it.

When written feedback arrives later, add it with its source and date. Ask how it changes the preparation for the next stage. Keep your own impressions useful but distinct from an employer's stated feedback.

5. Compare offers and decide what suits you

Use a chat called Offers and decision. Start with your priorities and the current facts for each opportunity. Compare actual written terms rather than assumptions from a call. You can anonymise employers and share selected terms rather than uploading a full contract.

Help me compare the offers or opportunities below against my own
priorities. Ask me which factors matter most before recommending
questions or trade-offs. Do not decide or accept anything for me.

Compare actual salary and hours, contract type, location and commute,
flexibility, responsibilities, development, benefits, start date,
probation, offer conditions and decision deadline where supplied.
Keep full-time equivalent and actual part-time pay separate.
Distinguish a written offer, a verbal indication and an interview
still in progress. Mark unknown terms and suggest questions to ask.

Show the trade-offs, missing evidence and how my preference could
change if an uncertain detail changes. Do not invent terms or treat
my interview impressions as established facts about the workplace.
Prepare any follow-up or acceptance message as a draft only.

Use the comparison to organise your thinking, then check the terms and make your own decision. For contractual, financial or immigration questions with significant consequences, use appropriate qualified support. Record an acceptance, decline or withdrawal only once you have actually taken that step yourself.

Keep the loop going

Prepare → have the conversation → debrief → check the summary → update the overview → prepare for the next step.

Keep all related work organised in your own Project while sharing only necessary information. It is not a confidential vault or a perfect record. Read the privacy notes, keep your own final documents and never put your real job-search records in this public demo repository.
